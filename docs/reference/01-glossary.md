## 1. Rules

1.  **One term, one meaning.** If a word has two meanings, one of them
    gets another word. Synonyms in the *Avoid* column must not be used
    in specifications, UI or code.
2.  **English terms are the reference.** Entity names, statuses,
    permissions and IDs use the English term, as they appear in code
    and APIs. Specifications may be written in French but use these
    terms for entities.
3.  **French and Arabic terms are for people.** They are used in the UI,
    generated documents and notifications.
4.  **New terms are added here first.** A cahier that introduces a term
    adds it to this glossary in the same change.
5.  **Statuses** (e.g. `ACTIVE`, `VERIFIED`) are defined in the state
    machine catalog (DM-04), not here.

**Arabic column:** first draft. It must be reviewed by a native speaker
who knows Tunisian real-estate and legal usage before it is used in the
product. Tunisian contracts often use terms that differ from Modern
Standard Arabic (for example المتسوّغ for tenant, الكراء for rent,
الخلاص for payment); where both exist, the Tunisian term is listed
first.

## 2. Naming decisions

These settle the collisions found in Phase 0 and CdC 00 (documentation
map, findings F-04 and F-12). **Status: proposed — to confirm.**

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| N-01 | **Tenant** means only the *locataire*. A customer company of the SaaS is an **Organization**. In code and data: `organization_id`, never `tenant_id`. "Multi-tenant" is used only as an architecture adjective, meaning "many organizations, strictly isolated". | "Tenant" was used for both; mixing them in the data model would be a permanent source of bugs. |
| N-02 | **Owner** means only a property owner. The person who runs an organization's account is the **Organization Administrator**. | "Owner" was ambiguous between property owner and account owner. |
| N-03 | When the organization owns units it sells (developer, or agency reselling), it is recorded as the **Owner** of those units. | Keeps one ownership model for both seller types (D-008). |
| N-04 | **Vendor** is the external company or independent professional. Avoid "Vendor Organization" (Phase 0 §0.23): **Organization** is reserved for customer companies. | Avoids a third meaning of "organization". |
| N-05 | **Invoice** is issued *by* the organization (a receivable). **Vendor Bill** is received *from* a vendor (a payable). Never "invoice" alone for a vendor's document. | Phase 0 and CdC 00 used "Invoice" for both, with different statuses. |
| N-06 | **Charge** is an amount due generated by a lease or a sale, before invoicing. **Expense** is a cost borne for a property. **Recoverable Charges** are the *charges locatives* re-billed to tenants. In French, "charge" is used only in "charges locatives". | In French, "charge" means both an amount due and a cost. |
| N-07 | **Maintenance Request** is the reported problem. **Work Order** is the job planned to fix it. | Keeps the two lifecycles separate. |
| N-08 | **Prospect** is someone interested; a **Buyer** has made an offer or reservation. **Seller** is a role (an Owner or the Organization), not a separate record. | Matches Phase 0 §0.3 and D-008. |
| N-09 | **Party** (*Tiers*) is any person or company the organization deals with. One party can play several roles (owner, tenant, buyer). | Whether this is one shared record is decided in DM-03. |
| N-10 | In French, **Bien** is used for a property, not "Propriété", which also means ownership. | Avoids confusion with ownership (*droit de propriété*). |
| N-11 | Avoid **client** / حريف in specifications: it can mean an organization, a tenant or a buyer. Use the precise term. | Same collision as "tenant". |
